Citrusdal → Tulbagh

9-day farm-to-farm hike through the Olifants River valley, over the Middelberg Pass and across the Koue Bokkeveld, ending at the NG Kerk in Tulbagh. Eight walking days and one rest day.

How accurate is this map? It's a reconstruction from the route booklet, not a recorded GPS track. Solid lines follow roads and tracks that are on OpenStreetMap. Dashed lines are drawn by hand where the trail crosses farmland or mountain with no mapped path (most of all the Witzenberg crossing on Day 9). Places tagged mapped come from OpenStreetMap; ones tagged estimated are placed from the booklet. Distances and climbs are about ±10–15%. On the ground, always follow the red lighthouse waymarks and the booklet.

Day by day

Waymarks

Follow white signs on poles, fences, gates and trees with a red lighthouse and a red arrow. On Day 7 there are also red arrows painted on rocks. The booklet mentions a sign at almost every turn. If you go more than a few hundred metres without seeing one, turn back to the last sign.

Planning & safety

  • Private land: nearly the whole route crosses working farms. Stay on the marked route, don't pick fruit or damage trees, close every gate, and don't disturb the people who live there.
  • Road walking: about a third of the route is on tar, including 18 km on the Middelberg Pass (Day 2), which has heavy trucks. Walk on the shoulder or on the gravel beside the tar, in single file, and wear something bright. Avoid walking at dusk.
  • Bees: there are hives in the orchards around Tweefontein (Day 6). If you're allergic, carry your medication.
  • Water: there are no shops between Citrusdal and Op die Berg. Carry at least 2–3 litres a day, more in summer. Ask your hosts to refill you each morning.
  • Food along the way: Citrusdal (Day 1), dried fruit at Kunje (Day 3, ask at the office), and Sip Café at Slagboom (end of Day 8). Plan everything else with your hosts.
  • Weather: the Koue Bokkeveld sits at 900–1,100 m and is one of the coldest places in the Western Cape, with frost and sometimes snow in winter (Jun–Aug). Summer days are hot (often 30 °C+) with little shade on the orchard roads. Spring (Sep–Oct) and autumn (Mar–May) are the most comfortable.
  • Cell signal: patchy on the plateau and over the mountains. Download offline maps and the GPX before you go, and tell someone your daily plan.
  • Fitness: 15–25 km a day for eight days, mostly on easy gradients. The big climbs are the Middelberg Pass (Day 2, about +750 m), the zig-zag cycle path (Day 6), and the long descent into Tulbagh (Day 9, about −900 m).
  • Getting home: the organisers can arrange transport from Tulbagh back to Citrusdal. Book it in advance.
  • Contacts: the hosts' and farm owners' phone numbers are in the printed booklet. They are left off this public page on purpose. Carry the booklet.
